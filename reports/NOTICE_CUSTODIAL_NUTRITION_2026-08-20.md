## 0. What this notice is — and what it is not

**It is not** a claim that the Third Geneva Convention legally governs Canadian civilian prisoners. It does not. Civilian detainees are governed by the *Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms*, the *Corrections and Conditional Release Act*, provincial corrections statutes, the *International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights* (ICCPR), the UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners (Mandela Rules), and, for Indigenous people, the Honour of the Crown.

**It is** this proposition, which a court can try and a minister cannot laugh off:

> Canada freely accepted, as binding federal law, a **results-based** food standard for captured enemy soldiers: rations sufficient in quantity, quality, and variety to keep them in good health and to **prevent loss of weight** and **nutritional deficiencies**. Canada’s own armed forces are trained to feed **all detained persons** to that standard, and **at least as well as Canadian Forces members eat**.  
>  
> A Canadian citizen in a provincial jail — often **not yet convicted of anything** — is fed on a paper menu designed for an inactive male, from a frozen cook-chill budget the Correctional Investigator has spent a decade calling inadequate, under an audit trail of spoilage, missed Food Guide days, and commissary dependence.  
>  
> If Canada were invaded tomorrow, the foreign troops we captured would be entitled, **by Canadian law and Canadian military doctrine**, to a food floor our own remanded citizens do not receive. That inversion is not a slogan. It is a *Charter* s. 7, s. 12, and s. 15 problem, an ICCPR Article 10 problem, and — because the people in those cells include women of reproductive age, disproportionately Indigenous — a transgenerational biology problem the epigenetic literature has already measured.

The invasion hypothetical is the **moral** frame. The **legal** frame is *a fortiori*: a state that bound itself to a health-preserving food floor for enemy combatants cannot, consistent with security of the person and equality, feed presumed-innocent Canadian citizens below that floor and then plead poverty, “Food Guide compliance,” or ignorance of the science.

## 1. The treaty floor Canada wrote onto its own statute book

### 1.1 Geneva Convention III, Article 26 (food)

Canada signed the four Geneva Conventions on 8 December 1949. It deposited its instrument of ratification on **14 May 1965**. The Conventions entered into force for Canada on 14 November 1965. They are given effect by the *Geneva Conventions Act*, R.S.C. 1985, c. G-3.

Article 26 is not a calorie slogan. It is a **results** test:

> The basic daily food rations shall be sufficient in quantity, quality and variety to keep prisoners of war in good health and to **prevent loss of weight** or the **development of nutritional deficiencies**. Account shall also be taken of the habitual diet of the prisoners.  
> The Detaining Power shall supply prisoners of war who work with such additional rations as are necessary for the labour on which they are employed.  
> Collective disciplinary measures affecting food are prohibited.

**Primary text:** ICRC, *Geneva Convention (III) relative to the Treatment of Prisoners of War*, 12 August 1949, Article 26.  
https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/gciii-1949/article-26

The ICRC Commentaries (1960 and 2020) are explicit: the “basic” daily ration is the **minimum required to keep prisoners in good health**; it must be provided **in full and in all circumstances**; outside parcels do not reduce the Detaining Power’s duty (Article 15 maintenance). Climate, age, health, and work must be taken into account. The test is not whether a dietitian signed a spreadsheet. The test is whether people **lose weight** or **develop deficiencies**.

**That is the floor Canada accepted for enemy soldiers.**

### 1.2 Canada’s own military doctrine — the smoking admission

The Canadian Forces *Code of Conduct*, Rule 6, is published by the Department of National Defence. It is not activist literature. It is how Canada trains its troops:

> Treat All Detained Persons Humanely in Accordance With the Standard Set by the Third Geneva Convention. Any Form of Abuse, Including Torture, Is Prohibited.

At the tactical level, **legal status does not matter**. Prisoners of war **and** detainees “shall be treated in accordance with the standard set by the Third Geneva Convention.”

On food, water, and shelter, the Code is more specific still:

> Food and water will be provided as soon as is feasible and will not be arbitrarily or unreasonably withheld. For example, **if CF personnel are scheduled to eat at a certain time, PWs and detainees will be allowed to eat at that time as well.**  
> … For the provision of food, water and shelter, the idea is **not to treat PWs or detainees better than CF members but to treat them at least as well.**

**Primary text:** Government of Canada, *Chapter 2: The Canadian Forces Code of Conduct*, Rule 6 (updated 7 October 2024).  
https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/reports-publications/military-law/code-conduct-cf/ch2-cf-code-conduct.html

Canadian Armed Forces Individual Meal Packs are specified at approximately **3,600 kcal per day** for operational activity (three IMPs). That is the field ration a captured enemy soldier is measured against when CF members are eating field rations.

**The invasion thought-experiment is therefore not rhetoric.** It is a statement of Canada’s own published doctrine. Captured foreign troops in CF custody eat when Canadian troops eat, at least as well, under a treaty that forbids weight loss and deficiencies. Canadian citizens in provincial remand eat cook-chill from a frozen per-diem, on a paper menu built for an **inactive** male, while the ombudsman tables reports about portions, palatability, and a black-market canteen economy.

## 2. What Canadian inmates actually get — the public record, not a vibe

### 2.1 The paper menu is not the meal

Correctional Service of Canada (CSC) Commissioner’s Directive Guideline 880-1 (*Food services — Central Feeding*) requires meals that “comply with the standards of Canada’s Food Guides … and are within allotted budgets.” The budget “is based on the daily rate established for the provision of three meals per inmate according to the **minimal** requirements of Canada’s Food Guides.” Cook-chill regional production — food cooked, bagged, shipped, stored, reheated — is the default industrial method since the 2014 Food Services Modernization Initiative.

**Primary text:** CSC Guideline 880-1 (26 June 2019).  
https://www.canada.ca/en/correctional-service/corporate/acts-regulations-policy/commissioners-directives/guidelines/880-01.html

CSC’s public claim is a National Menu of about **2,600 kcal/day**, calibrated to an **inactive male aged 31–50**. That is the number the Correctional Investigator has been repeating to Parliament since at least 2017.

A 2022 nutritional analysis of the National Menu (Johnson et al., *Nutrients*) found a paper offering of about **2,824 kcal**. The same paper found **sodium at 3,404 mg** against a Tolerable Upper Intake Level of 2,300 mg, ω-6 below Adequate Intake, and vitamin D below target for people over 70. The authors concluded the **menu offering** was “nutritionally adequate” **on paper**, while noting the “complex nature of prison food.”

**Primary study:** Johnson C. et al. (2022), “The Menu Served in Canadian Penitentiaries: A Nutritional Analysis,” *Nutrients* 14(16):3400. PMC9416739.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9416739/

**That paper is the Crown’s best exhibit.** It does not close this file. It measures the **menu**, not the **tray**. The gap between menu and tray is where the Charter claim lives.

### 2.2 The audit trail the Crown already wrote against itself

- **2014:** Food Services Modernization / cook-chill. Daily food budget frozen. Correctional Investigator Ivan Zinger told Parliament the freeze produced a **$5.41 per inmate per day** food budget, small portions, inferior quality, and a canteen black market. Each offender’s meals were said to provide 2,600 calories — “the guideline for an inactive male aged 31–50.” Zinger noted that younger, active men require more, and that his office estimated high rates of obesity and diabetes inside — a population for whom a paper calorie count is not a health result.  
  **Source:** CBC News, 31 October 2017, reporting the OCI annual report.  
  https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/correctional-investigator-zinger-report-1.4379823

- **2019 CSC Internal Audit of Food Services:** the National Menu did **not** meet Canada’s Food Guide **21% of the time** (6 of 28 days). Institutions prepared far more meals than required, then forbade storage. A third of institutions had spoiled or expired food in freezers. One institution threw away all excess after a sitting — a third of what was prepared. CBC reported the audit flagged the risk of a “food-related health event.”  
  **Sources:** CSC Internal Audit of Food Services (2019); CBC, 8 June 2019; Wilson A. (2023), *Studies in Social Justice*.  
  https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/prison-food-csc-audit-1.5167222

- **OCI complaint spike:** food-service contacts to the Correctional Investigator rose after 2014. The 2023–24 Annual Report still lists Food and Diet inside the largest complaint cluster (Conditions/Routine). The 2024–25 Annual Report — tabled 30 October 2025 — records that the Office “boldly raised issues of fairness” including **“the quality of prison food and the rising cost of living.”**  
  **Sources:** https://oci-bec.gc.ca/en/content/office-correctional-investigator-annual-report-2023-24  
  https://oci-bec.gc.ca/en/content/office-correctional-investigator-annual-report-2024-25

- **Senate and qualitative record:** Wilson (2022, 2023) and Knudsen (2025) document the post-2014 collapse in palatability, the shift of real calories onto the **canteen**, and the fact that people without outside money eat the tray. A menu that “meets the Food Guide” on a spreadsheet is not Article 26 compliance if the tray is inedible and the deficiency is outsourced to a store.

**The Geneva test is weight and deficiencies, not a signed menu.** CSC has not published a national, independent, results-based audit of inmate weight change and micronutrient status against Article 26’s criteria. That omission, after a decade of ombudsman warnings, is itself evidence.

### 2.3 Jails are not penitentiaries — and they are worse on the facts that matter

“Jail” in Canada means **provincial and territorial** custody: remand (unconvicted), sentences under two years, immigration holds in some systems. The majority of people in provincial custody on a given day are **awaiting trial**. They carry the presumption of innocence. They are not “POWs.” They are **citizens the state has not yet convicted**.

Ontario’s provincial system was feeding about 8,000 people a day; 2016 food-service spend was reported at **$9.17 per prisoner per day** (Montford, 2022). A higher dollar figure than the federal freeze does not rescue the file. Montford’s title is the finding: *“The Embodiment of Contempt: Ontario Provincial Prison Food.”* Quality, industrial production, and contempt-as-policy are the issue, not a race to the cheapest per-diem.

**Primary study:** Montford K.S. et al. (2022), *Journal of Prisoners on Prisons* / PMC9996774.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9996774/

Provincial ombudsman reports, lockdown meal bags, cancelled kitchen programs, and commissary dependence are the provincial analogue of the federal cook-chill file. **Most of the people this notice is about are in provincial jails, not CSC penitentiaries.** Serving only Ottawa is a jurisdictional dodge. That is why every provincial minister of corrections and every provincial ombudsperson is on the service list.

### 2.4 Who is in the cells

OCI 2022–23 (and subsequent repeats): Indigenous people are about **5%** of the Canadian population and about **32%** of federal inmates. Indigenous women are about **50%** of federally incarcerated women. The Correctional Investigator has called this a crisis and a national disgrace.

Women of reproductive age are in federal and provincial custody. Pregnancy in custody is a documented operational fact, not a hypothetical. Folate, iron, iodine, vitamin D, and energy needs in the periconceptional window are not “special diets” to be bargained against a canteen. They are the one-carbon and micronutrient inputs the epigenetic studies below measured.

## 3. The epigenetic record — why this is not “prisoners complaining about lunch”

A minister who treats this as a comfort grievance is scientifically illiterate on a matter of **security of the person**, including the bodily integrity of children not yet born.

**Honest scope, stated first so it cannot be used to kill the rest:**

The Dutch Hunger Winter studies measured **prenatal** famine, not a 2,600 kcal paper menu served to adults. This notice does **not** claim that Canadian jail food has been assayed for IGF2 hypomethylation. It claims something a court can work with:

1. Nutritional deprivation around conception and early life **does** leave durable DNA-methylation marks with adult metabolic consequences. That is no longer a theory. It is a measured human finding.
2. The biochemical pathway — folate and related one-carbon donors feeding DNA methylation — is textbook.
3. Canada holds, in state custody, thousands of people of reproductive age, including a grossly disproportionate number of Indigenous women, and holds pregnant people.
4. After this notice, the Crown cannot say the transgenerational risk was unknown.

### 3.1 The human famine studies (the load-bearing citations)

**Heijmans B.T., Tobi E.W., Stein A.D., Putter H., Blauw G.J., Susser E.S., Slagboom P.E., Lumey L.H. (2008).** “Persistent epigenetic differences associated with prenatal exposure to famine in humans.” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 105(44): 17046–17049. doi:10.1073/pnas.0806560105. PMID 18955703.

Periconceptional exposure to the Dutch Hunger Winter (1944–45) was associated with **lower methylation of the imprinted IGF2 differentially methylated region**, still detectable **six decades later**, compared with unexposed same-sex siblings. Average methylation fraction 0.488 (exposed) vs 0.515 (unexposed); 5.2% lower methylation, *P* = 5.9 × 10⁻⁵. Late-gestation exposure did not show the same IGF2 mark. The critical window is **around conception**.

https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0806560105

**Tobi E.W. et al. (2009).** “DNA methylation differences after exposure to prenatal famine are common and timing- and sex-specific.” *Human Molecular Genetics*. Persistent methylation changes as a common consequence of prenatal famine, dependent on timing and sex.

**Tobi E.W. et al. (2014).** “DNA methylation signatures link prenatal famine exposure to growth and metabolism.” *Nature Communications* 5: 5592. Genome-scale differential methylation in whole blood after periconceptional famine exposure, including at metabolic loci (prior IGF2 and LEP findings extended).

**Roseboom T.J., Painter R.C., van Abeelen A.F., Veenendaal M.V., de Rooij S.R. (2011).** Dutch Famine Birth Cohort: prenatal undernutrition associated with later-life type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and other metabolic outcomes. The methylation papers are the mechanism under the epidemiology.

### 3.2 The pathway (so “epigenetics” is not a vibe word)

**Waterland R.A. & Jirtle R.L. (2003).** “Transposable elements: targets for early nutritional effects on epigenetic gene regulation.” *Molecular and Cellular Biology* 23(15): 5293–5300. Methyl-donor supplementation (folate, B12, choline, betaine) alters DNA methylation at the *Agouti* viable yellow locus and coat-colour/metabolic phenotype. The diet-to-methylation causal chain was demonstrated in a mammal.

**Tiffon C. (2018).** “The Impact of Nutrition and Environmental Epigenetics on Human Health and Disease.” *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* 19(11): 3425. PMC6275017. Review of dietary effects on DNA methylation, including folate metabolism as a core one-carbon input.

**Guo T. et al. (2020).** “You are affected by what your parents eat: Diet, epigenetics, and transgenerational/intergenerational inheritance.” *Trends in Food Science & Technology*. Malnutrition, hyper-nutrition, and specific-component deficiency all produce epigenetic alterations, some transmissible.

**Donkin I. et al. (2016).** “Obesity and Bariatric Surgery Drive Epigenetic Variation of Spermatozoa in Humans.” *Cell Metabolism* 23(2): 369–378. Paternal diet/metabolic state associated with sperm DNA methylation differences — the custody population is not only pregnant women.

### 3.3 What a responsible Crown does with this literature

It does **not** demand a Hunger-Winter calorie count in a Canadian jail before it acts. Article 26 already forbids nutritional deficiencies. The Mandela Rules already require food of nutritional value adequate for health and strength, wholesome, well prepared and served (Rule 22). *Charter* s. 7 already protects security of the person. The epigenetic literature tells the minister **why** a “Food Guide on paper / tray in the garbage / canteen if you have family money” regime is not a kitchen complaint. It is a biological-integrity regime imposed by the state on a captured population, including people who can conceive children in or after custody.

Indigenous over-incarceration converts this from a general s. 7 problem into a **s. 15** problem and an Honour-of-the-Crown problem. The people most exposed to the methylation-relevant window are the people Canada already over-imprisons.

## 4. The law that actually binds the people who will receive this

### 4.1 Charter

**Section 7.** Everyone has the right to life, liberty, and security of the person and the right not to be deprived thereof except in accordance with the principles of fundamental justice. People in custody are in the state’s exclusive care. Food is not a privilege. Inedible or deficient food, commissary-gated calories, and indifference to pregnancy and micronutrients are deprivations of **security of the person**. Transgenerational risk, once known, is not “speculative.” It is a reasoned inference from measured human studies plus a known custody population.

**Section 12.** Everyone has the right not to be subjected to any cruel and unusual treatment or punishment. Canadian courts already used the Mandela Rules as interpretive aids when they struck down administrative segregation (*Canadian Civil Liberties Association* / *British Columbia Civil Liberties Association* litigation). Food that systematically fails a results test — weight loss, deficiencies, inedible industrial product — is treatment. After notice, continuation is **knowing** treatment.

**Section 15.** Equality before and under the law. Indigenous people, women, people with diabetes and other diet-related illness, and people too poor for the canteen are not identically situated to the “inactive male 31–50” the National Menu was built for. A single paper calorie count that ignores those groups is a distinction with a documented effect.

**Section 24(1) damages.** *Vancouver (City) v. Ward*, 2010 SCC 27; *Henry v. British Columbia (Attorney General)*, 2015 SCC 24. After this notice, continued indifference is available as evidence of **callous disregard**. That is the damages multiplier. That is why this is served as a notice and not a blog post.

### 4.2 Statute the ministers already administer

**Corrections and Conditional Release Act**, S.C. 1992, c. 20:

- s. 3 — purpose includes assisting the rehabilitation of offenders and their reintegration **as law-abiding citizens** (a starved, metabolically injured person is not the statutory product).
- s. 4 — principles: least restrictive, humane, and taking into account the special needs of women, Indigenous persons, and persons requiring mental-health care.
- **s. 70** — inmates shall be provided with “living conditions that are **safe, healthful** and that meet standards of hygiene.”
- **s. 86** — CSC shall provide every inmate with essential health care and reasonable access to non-essential health care. Nutrition is not a canteen option adjacent to health care. It is health care’s substrate.

**Commissioner’s Directives** cannot lawfully shrink s. 70 and s. 86 to “minimal Food Guide within allotted budgets” when the allotted budget is the freeze the ombudsman has been describing since 2014.

**Provincial corrections statutes** impose equivalent duties of care on provincial jails. The unconvicted remand majority makes the provincial duty **stricter**, not looser: the state has not even obtained a conviction to point to.

### 4.3 International law that Canada did accept for civilians

**ICCPR Article 10(1):** “All persons deprived of their liberty shall be treated with humanity and with respect for the inherent dignity of the human person.” Canada is a party. Canada accepted the **First Optional Protocol**. After domestic remedies are exhausted **or shown to be unreasonably prolonged**, individuals may communicate to the UN Human Rights Committee. A decade of OCI reports without a results-based food fix is the “unreasonably prolonged” record.

**ICCPR Article 7** (cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment) sits behind s. 12.

**Mandela Rules, Rule 22:** every prisoner shall be provided, at the usual hours, with food of nutritional value adequate for health and strength, of wholesome quality, **well prepared and served**. Drinking water available at all times. Canadian courts have already treated these Rules as interpretive aids. Rule 22 is the civilian twin of Article 26. The “well prepared and served” clause is where cook-chill and contempt fail even if a spreadsheet hits 2,600 kcal.

**UN Convention against Torture.** Canada is a party. Canada has **not** ratified the Optional Protocol (OPCAT) — the CHRC and the OCI issued an open letter on 1 November 2023 calling for ratification. That failure is not a defence. It is an admission that Canada refuses the preventive-visiting mechanism designed for places of detention. CAT Article 16 (cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment) still applies. The UN Special Rapporteur on Torture may receive communications regardless.

**Honour of the Crown / UNDRIP Act.** Indigenous women at 50% of federally incarcerated women is not a demographic footnote. It is the equality and Honour overlay on every tray.

Provincial *Crown Liability and Proceedings* statutes commonly require **written notice** before certain claims. This document is that notice, as against every provincial Crown named in the service list, for claims arising from custodial nutrition, including *Charter* damages.

Federally, *Charter* claims against the Crown do not depend on a statutory notice period. Service still does this:

- It **starts discoverability** for anyone who reads it.

- It is admissible as evidence of knowledge in *Ward* / *Henry* damages, in OCI investigations, in SECU testimony, in an ICCPR Optional Protocol communication, and in class-action certification on the common issue: *does the served diet meet a results-based health-preserving standard?*

## 5. The demands — specific enough that “under review” is a refusal

Reply **in writing within 30 days** of service. Accept or refuse each numbered demand, with reasons and a named official. Silence on a numbered demand is a **refusal on the record**.

**Demand 1 — Disclose the results, not the menu.** Within 30 days, CSC and each provincial/territorial corrections ministry shall publish (or produce to the Correctional Investigator / provincial ombudsperson, with a public summary):

- per-inmate per-day food expenditure for the last 36 months, in current dollars;
- the last 12 months of **menu versus served-portion** audits, if they exist, and a sworn statement if they do not;
- available inmate **weight-change** data;
- any micronutrient or diet-related clinical data already in health files (anonymized);
- canteen food-revenue figures, so the public can see how much of “nutrition” has been privatized onto families.

**Demand 2 — Independent results audit.** Within 60 days, commission an independent registered-dietitian and public-health audit whose terms of reference are **Article 26 / Mandela Rule 22 results** (weight maintenance, absence of deficiencies, palatability/consumption, pregnancy and illness), **not** “does the spreadsheet match the Food Guide.” Publish the terms of reference immediately.

**Demand 3 — Perinatal floor, immediately.** Pregnant, postpartum, and trying-to-conceive inmates receive Health Canada prenatal nutrition standards — energy, folate, iron, iodine, vitamin D, calcium — as a **duty**, not a request form. The inactive-male 2,600 kcal menu is not a perinatal diet. This demand is effective on receipt.

**Demand 4 — Equalize to Canada’s own detainee doctrine.** Within 90 days, adopt a custodial feeding rule no lower than CAF Code of Conduct Rule 6: people in custody eat at the same times as staff, at a quality that prevents weight loss and deficiencies, without canteen means-testing. If Canada can train 19-year-old infantry to do this for captured enemy combatants, it can instruct a kitchen manager to do it for remanded Canadians.

**Demand 5 — Kill the 2014 freeze as policy.** Within 180 days, replace the “minimal Food Guide within allotted budgets” instruction with a **health-outcome** instruction, and end cook-chill as the default wherever the 2019 audit conditions (spoilage, overproduction-and-dump, inedible reheats) persist.

**Demand 6 — Indigenous-specific nutrition.** Within 90 days, table a plan, with NWAC and other Indigenous governing bodies invited, for culturally adequate, nutritionally adequate food in custody, including ceremonial and habitual-diet requirements that Article 26 itself names as a legal concept (“habitual diet of the prisoners”).

**Demand 7 — OPCAT.** The Minister of Public Safety shall, within 90 days, state in writing whether Canada will ratify the Optional Protocol to the Convention against Torture, as the CHRC and OCI requested in 2023, and if not, why a country that will not allow preventive detention visits expects to be believed on kitchen claims.
