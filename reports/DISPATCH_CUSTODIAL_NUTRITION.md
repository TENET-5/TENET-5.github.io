## Cover A — Minister / Commissioner / Attorney General (Tier 0)

**Subject:** Notice of Constitutional Claim and Continuing Breach — custodial nutrition below Canada’s own prisoner-of-war food floor

Sir / Madam:

Please find enclosed a Notice of Constitutional Claim dated 20 August 2026, also published at the URL above.

The Notice does **not** assert that the Third Geneva Convention governs civilian prisoners. It asserts that Canada bound itself, as federal law and as Canadian Forces doctrine (Code of Conduct Rule 6), to a **results-based** food standard for detained enemy combatants — quantity, quality, and variety sufficient to prevent weight loss and nutritional deficiencies, meals at least as good as those issued to Canadian Forces members — and that Canadian citizens in federal penitentiaries and provincial jails, including the unconvicted remand majority, are held below that floor on the Crown’s own audits and ombudsman record.

The Notice cites the epigenetic literature (Heijmans et al., *PNAS* 2008; Tobi et al., 2009, 2014; Waterland & Jirtle, 2003) with an honest scope: prenatal famine is not a 2,600 kcal paper menu. It is, however, measured proof that nutritional deprivation around conception leaves durable biological marks. Canada holds women of reproductive age in custody, disproportionately Indigenous women. After this letter, that risk is **known** to your office.

**You are asked to accept or refuse, in writing, each of Demands 1–7 in the Notice within 30 days.** Silence on a numbered demand will be treated as a refusal on the public record.

This letter is also notice, as against the federal Crown, of facts material to *Charter* ss. 7, 12 and 15, *CCRA* ss. 70 and 86, and ICCPR Articles 7 and 10.

Respectfully,  
TENET5 Investigative Newsroom

## Cover B — Office of the Correctional Investigator (statutory complaint)

**Subject:** Complaint and request for a special report — custodial nutrition versus results-based standards (Geneva III Art. 26 / Mandela Rule 22 / CAF Rule 6)

Pursuant to your mandate under Part III of the *Corrections and Conditional Release Act*, please treat the enclosed Notice as a complaint concerning food services in federal corrections, and as a request that your Office:

1. Investigate whether the **served** diet (not the National Menu spreadsheet) meets a results-based standard of health, weight maintenance, and absence of deficiencies;
2. Compare CSC practice with CAF Code of Conduct Rule 6 and with Mandela Rule 22;
3. Consider a **special report** on custodial nutrition, including perinatal inmates and Indigenous women;
4. Seek the 2019 Internal Audit working papers and all subsequent menu-compliance audits.

The 2024–25 Annual Report already records your Office’s concern with “the quality of prison food.” This complaint asks you to convert that concern into a results audit the minister cannot file under “noted.”

## Cover D — Clerk of SECU

**Subject:** Request that the Committee order papers on custodial nutrition versus CAF detainee feeding doctrine

The Committee has the Minister of Public Safety, the CSC Commissioner, and the Correctional Investigator within its orbit. A single round of questions, on the record, would be:

> Minister, the Canadian Forces Code of Conduct Rule 6 requires that all detained persons eat when our troops eat, at least as well, to the Third Geneva Convention standard. The Correctional Investigator has reported a frozen per-diem, cook-chill, and inadequate portions for Canadian inmates. If Canada were holding captured foreign soldiers tomorrow, which standard would apply — Rule 6, or the inmate kitchen? Why is the lower standard the one we use on remanded Canadian citizens?

Please circulate the enclosed Notice to members as a brief.
