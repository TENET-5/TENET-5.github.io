Five Documented Failures

Failure 1: National Emergency Strategic Stockpile — Depleted and Expired

The AG found that Canada's National Emergency Strategic Stockpile was depleted and contained expired equipment before COVID-19 hit. PPE, ventilators, and medical supplies were insufficient. Emergency procurement from China produced quality concerns and delivery delays. The stockpile failure meant Canada entered the worst health emergency in a century without basic medical supplies.

Supply chain analysis →

Failure 2: Pharmaceutical Dependence — No Domestic Manufacturing

80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients from China and India. Both countries restricted exports during COVID. Canada had zero domestic API manufacturing capacity. The pharmaceutical vulnerability documented in the pharma dependence analysis was exposed in real time — and has not been adequately addressed since.

Failure 3: ArriveCAN — $59M Procurement Scandal

An app estimated at $80,000 cost $59 million. The AG documented significant procurement irregularities. ArriveCAN also established mandatory digital credential infrastructure at borders — the digital identity architecture that persists beyond the pandemic. The procurement failure and the surveillance infrastructure it created are both documented consequences.

ArriveCAN dossier →

Failure 4: MAID Expanded During Crisis — Bill C-7 (2021)

During a global health emergency, the government expanded MAID to individuals whose death was not reasonably foreseeable. Bill C-7 passed while hospitals were overwhelmed, healthcare workers burned out, and the healthcare system was under maximum stress. The expansion of state-sanctioned death during a healthcare crisis is documented in the Hansard record — MPs voted to expand killing while the system that should have been healing was collapsing.

MAID expansion analysis →

Failure 5: Emergencies Act — Ruled Unreasonable

The government invoked the Emergencies Act for the first time in Canadian history. 210+ bank accounts frozen without court orders. The Federal Court ruled the invocation unreasonable and found Charter violations — two years later. The Emergencies Act analysis documents how emergency powers were used for political objectives while the court ruling arrived too late to function as accountability.
