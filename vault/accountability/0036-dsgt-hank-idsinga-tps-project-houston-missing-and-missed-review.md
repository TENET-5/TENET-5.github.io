# DSgt Hank Idsinga (TPS) — Project Houston / Missing and Missed Review

**DOCUMENTED INSTITUTIONAL FAILURE** · 2012–2018

## Detail

Justice Gloria Epstein's Independent Civilian Review (Missing and Missed, April 13, 2021) made specific named findings on Project Houston, the disbanded TPS task force investigating disappearances later linked to serial killer Bruce McArthur. The Review found that Detective Sergeant Idsinga and Detective Constable Loria "recognized the significance" of identifying the subscriber of the email/username silverfoxx51 (in fact, McArthur) — yet the task "remained unaddressed for many months — and was addressed only after both Detective Constable Loria and Detective Sergeant Idsinga had left the project." A separate Review finding documented that McArthur's November 2013 Project Houston interview was never uploaded into PowerCase, with the result that "extensive information collected by officers was often never used"; Project Prism investigators had to re-discover McArthur from scratch. Project Houston was disbanded April 2014. McArthur killed four more men before his January 2018 arrest: Soroush Mahmudi (Aug 2015), Dean Lisowick (May 2016 – July 2017), Selim Esen (April 2017), and Andrew Kinsman (June 2017). Idsinga's 2025 memoir The High Road: Confessions of a Homicide Cop (Simon & Schuster), publicly framed as institutional whistleblowing on TPS senior leadership for racism, antisemitism, alcoholism, and cronyism, does not engage with the Review's named findings against him personally; he declined TPS Professional Standards' April 2025 invitation to participate in a formal investigation of his own memoir's allegations. Sources: Missing and Missed, Independent Civilian Review (TPSB, 2021-04-13); Globe and Mail; CBC News; Toronto Life; [missing-and-missed-review.html](missing-and-missed-review.html).

## Outcome

OUTCOME: 151 Review recommendations. Approximately 10% TPS-implemented as of Feb 2023. No criminal charges, no disciplinary action against Idsinga personally; Review treats the failures as institutional. Four McArthur victims killed in the gap between Project Houston disbandment (Apr 2014) and McArthur arrest (Jan 2018). Idsinga retired late 2023, published memoir 2025, declined formal TPS investigation of his own allegations.
