Lux Leaks. November 5, 2014. 28,000 pages of confidential tax rulings leaked from PricewaterhouseCoopers Luxembourg. Revealed that 340 multinational companies secured secret tax deals with Luxembourg, paying effective rates below 1 percent. Canadian companies including Brookfield, Power Corporation, and major banks used these arrangements. Antoine Deltour, the whistleblower, was convicted under Luxembourg law. The European Commission launched state aid investigations.


Swiss Leaks. February 8, 2015. More than 60,000 leaked files from HSBC's Swiss private banking arm exposed 106,000 clients from 203 countries holding 100 billion dollars in assets. CRA identified 1,859 Canadian HSBC clients with accounts totalling 5.7 billion dollars. Only one criminal prosecution resulted. HSBC paid no criminal penalty in Canada despite facilitating tax evasion on a massive scale.


Panama Papers. April 3, 2016. 11.5 million documents leaked from Mossack Fonseca, a Panamanian law firm. 214,488 offshore entities across 21 jurisdictions. 912 Canadian entities, 242 intermediaries, 1,312 Canadian addresses. CRA identified nearly 900 Canadians. Only 5 investigations opened. RCMP sought access but no criminal charges resulted.


Paradise Papers. November 5, 2017. 13.4 million documents from Appleby law firm. More than 3,000 Canadian companies and individuals named. 7 of Canada's 8 major pension funds using offshore tax havens for retirement savings. Stephen Bronfman, Liberal Party chief fundraiser, linked to Cayman Islands trust. Zero criminal investigations launched.


FinCEN Files. September 20, 2020. More than 2,100 suspicious activity reports filed with the US Financial Crimes Enforcement Network were leaked. These reports flagged 2 trillion dollars in suspicious transactions between 1999 and 2017. Canadian banks including TD Bank, Bank of Montreal, and Scotiabank processed hundreds of millions in flagged transactions. Despite being reported as suspicious, the transactions were completed. No Canadian bank faced enforcement action.


Pandora Papers. October 3, 2021. 11.9 million documents from 14 offshore service providers. 35 current and former world leaders named. Canadian pension fund investments documented routing through offshore vehicles. Canadians for Tax Fairness called it a global network of which Canada is a hub. Still zero criminal prosecutions.


Suisse Secrets. February 20, 2022. An anonymous source leaked data on more than 18,000 accounts at Credit Suisse holding over 100 billion Swiss francs. The accounts belonged to heads of state, intelligence officials, sanctioned individuals, and those accused of human rights abuses. Canadian-linked accounts were among those exposed. Credit Suisse collapsed in 2023 and was absorbed by UBS in an emergency takeover. No Canadian regulatory action followed.


Cyprus Confidential. November 2023. Leaked documents from six Cypriot financial service providers revealed how Cyprus became Europe's primary money laundering hub for Russian oligarchs and sanctioned individuals. Billions in assets were sheltered through Cypriot shell companies even after the 2022 sanctions on Russia. Canadian entities with cross-border ties to Cypriot structures were documented. The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority was established in part due to these revelations. Canada took no action.


CRA enforcement failure. After eight major leaks across a decade, the Canada Revenue Agency identified 76 million dollars in unpaid taxes from just two of them. They cannot or will not confirm whether they collected a single dollar. Globally, these leaks led to more than 1.36 billion dollars in recovered taxes. Canada: zero criminal prosecutions, zero confirmed collections.


The Carney Brookfield FINTRAC pipeline. In 2026, as the Carney Government and Brookfield began routing Canadian pension funds through offshore accounts, Carney slashed funding to FINTRAC, the agency responsible for tracking offshore money laundering. The PM holds 6.8 million dollars in Brookfield options. Brookfield manages 1 trillion globally. FINTRAC budget cut 12 percent. 47 analyst positions eliminated.
