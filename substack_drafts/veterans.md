How Canada Treats Its Veterans — The Data. Higher suicide rates. Months-long waits for disability benefits. And when they ask for a wheelchair ramp, they're offered death. Every number below comes from the Government of Canada's own reports.


Veteran suicide. Male veterans die by suicide at 1.4 times the civilian rate. Female veterans at 1.9 times. This elevated risk has persisted for forty-two years. The government has known.


The disability benefits backlog. Only forty-seven percent of applications now meet the sixteen-week standard. The backlog doubled from five thousand six hundred to over eleven thousand in a single year.


Mental health claim denials. Of fifteen thousand veterans who applied for mental health disability benefits, twenty-four percent were denied. Nearly one in four told their suffering does not qualify.


MAID — offered death instead of help. A Paralympian veteran asked for a wheelchair ramp. The government's response: we can help you die. At least four to five confirmed cases of veterans being offered MAID by a Veterans Affairs employee.


The math. A MAID procedure costs twenty-five hundred dollars. A wheelchair ramp costs three to eight thousand. Annual disability benefits cost fifteen to forty thousand per veteran. It is cheaper to kill a veteran than to provide a ramp for two years.


My case. A veteran reported foreign interference targeting the mental health infrastructure that serves soldiers. The response was not investigation. It was prosecution. The message: stay silent or be destroyed.


Veteran homelessness. Three to five thousand Canadian veterans are homeless on any given night. Veterans are overrepresented in shelter populations by two to four times their proportion of the general population.


The veteran-to-MAID pipeline. Inadequate transition, denied claims, impossible wait times, and then an offer of death. The government's own data documents every link in this chain. This is not random failure. It is a connected pipeline.
