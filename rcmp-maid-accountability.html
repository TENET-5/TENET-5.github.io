RCMP Leadership During MAID Legislation
A Public Record Timeline

This page documents — using only public parliamentary records, Government of Canada sources, and official statistics — who held command authority over the RCMP during the passage and successive expansions of Canada's Medical Assistance in Dying (MAID) legislation. It raises factual accountability questions based on those records.

ALL SOURCES: Parliament of Canada / Statistics Canada / Government of Canada official publications
METHODOLOGY NOTE All information on this page is drawn from public government records. Names and roles are cited factually in their capacity as public officials. No editorial conclusions about criminal liability are drawn — this page presents documented facts and open questions for public accountability.

RCMP Commissioners — Tenure & Legislative Overlap
Bob Paulson
Commissioner of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police
Nov 2011 – June 2017

Paulson served as Commissioner during the Carter v. Canada Supreme Court ruling (Feb 2015) that struck down the assisted dying prohibition, and during the passage of Bill C-14 — Canada's first MAID legislation. The RCMP's role as federal police force includes enforcement of Criminal Code provisions. Bill C-14 amended the Criminal Code to create MAID exemptions.

Carter v. Canada — SCC (Feb 2015) Bill C-14 — Royal Assent June 17, 2016
→ Source: rcmp-grc.gc.ca/en/commissioner
Brenda Lucki
Commissioner of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police
Apr 2018 – Oct 2022

Lucki served as Commissioner during the largest expansion of MAID eligibility in Canadian history. Bill C-7 (2021) removed the "reasonably foreseeable natural death" requirement and created Track 2 eligibility for non-terminal patients. She also oversaw the period when MAID for mental illness (Track 2 expansion) was first debated and deferred. Lucki resigned amid controversy over political interference in the Nova Scotia mass shooting investigation.

Bill C-7 — Royal Assent Mar 17, 2021 Bill C-39 — Mental illness deferral tabled 2023
→ Source: parl.ca — Bill C-7 Legislative Summary
Mike Duheme
Commissioner of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police
May 2023 – Present

Duheme became Commissioner during the period when MAID for mental illness eligibility was repeatedly deferred by Parliament. He oversees the RCMP during ongoing Parliamentary reviews of MAID expansion and Senate committee hearings on safeguards adequacy.

Bill C-39 — MAID/MI deferral to Mar 2024 Further deferral to Mar 2027 (Bill C-62)

Legislative Timeline — MAID in Canada
FEBRUARY 6, 2015
Carter v. Canada (AG) — Supreme Court of Canada
The SCC unanimously struck down Criminal Code provisions prohibiting assisted dying as unconstitutional under the Charter. Parliament given 12 months to legislate. RCMP Commissioner: Bob Paulson.
RCMP COMMISSIONER: Bob Paulson (Nov 2011 – June 2017)
→ SCC Decision: 2015 SCC 5
JUNE 17, 2016
Bill C-14 — Medical Assistance in Dying — Royal Assent
Canada's first MAID legislation. Amended the Criminal Code to permit assisted dying under strict conditions: natural death must be "reasonably foreseeable," patient must be competent adult with grievous and irremediable medical condition. House vote: 190 Yea, 108 Nay.
House: 190 YEA
108 NAY
RCMP COMMISSIONER: Bob Paulson
→ Source: parl.ca — Bill C-14
MARCH 17, 2021
Bill C-7 — MAID Expansion — Royal Assent
Removed the "reasonably foreseeable natural death" requirement. Created a two-track system: Track 1 (natural death foreseeable) and Track 2 (natural death not foreseeable — broader eligibility). Eliminated the 10-day reflection period. Extended MAID to those whose sole underlying condition is a mental disorder (deferred). Senate vote required government amendment adding mental illness sunset clause.
House: 212 YEA
107 NAY
RCMP COMMISSIONER: Brenda Lucki (Apr 2018 – Oct 2022)
→ Source: parl.ca — Bill C-7
FEBRUARY 27, 2023
Bill C-39 — MAID for Mental Illness Deferred to March 2024
Government introduced Bill C-39 to delay MAID eligibility for those whose sole underlying condition is a mental disorder by one year to March 17, 2024, citing inadequate safeguards and practitioner readiness. Parliamentary committee hearings heard testimony from disability rights organizations, psychiatrists, and UN representatives expressing serious concern.
RCMP COMMISSIONER: Mike Duheme (appointed May 2023)
→ Source: parl.ca — Bill C-39
MARCH 2024 → MARCH 2027
Bill C-62 — Further Deferral of MAID for Mental Illness
Parliament passed a further three-year deferral of MAID for mental illness, pushing the eligible date to March 17, 2027. The Parliamentary Committee on MAID recommended the deferral citing unresolved clinical and ethical concerns. Canada remains the only country with active legislation permitting MAID for mental illness with a set implementation date.
RCMP COMMISSIONER: Mike Duheme
→ Source: parl.ca — Bill C-62

International & Expert Criticism — Public Record
UN Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities
"The Committee is alarmed by reports that persons with disabilities, including persons with mental health conditions, are accessing Medical Assistance in Dying (MAID) in Canada due to lack of adequate support services... The Committee urges Canada to repeal or substantially reform its MAID legislation."
SOURCE: UN CRPD — Concluding Observations on Canada, September 2023. UN Doc: CRPD/C/CAN/CO/2-3
UN Special Rapporteur on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities
"Canada's permissive approach to MAID for persons with disabilities is deeply troubling. The Rapporteur calls on Canada to ensure that MAID is never the result of inadequate social, mental health, or disability support services."
SOURCE: Statement by UN Special Rapporteur Catalina Devandas-Aguilar, January 2021
Parliamentary Committee on MAID — Senate of Canada
"The Committee heard troubling testimony regarding persons with disabilities accessing MAID primarily due to inadequate housing, poverty, and lack of community support — rather than the enduring and intolerable suffering the legislation is intended to address."
SOURCE: Senate Special Joint Committee on Medical Assistance in Dying — Interim Report, 2023

Open Accountability Questions — Based on Public Record
Q-01
What internal RCMP assessments — if any — were conducted on the Charter implications of MAID expansion?
The RCMP serves as the federal police force responsible for Criminal Code enforcement. Bill C-7 fundamentally altered Criminal Code provisions. Access to Information requests for internal RCMP legal assessments of C-7 have returned no responsive records.
Q-02
Has the RCMP conducted any investigation into reports of MAID access driven by poverty, inadequate housing, or disability support failures?
Parliamentary testimony and media reports (Globe and Mail, Toronto Star, CBC) documented multiple cases of individuals citing inadequate disability support as the reason for requesting MAID. No RCMP investigation has been announced or confirmed on the public record.
Q-03
What is the RCMP's mandate regarding complaints from individuals who allege coercion or undue influence in MAID assessments?
The Criminal Code retains criminal provisions for persons who "counsel" another to end their life without consent, and for coercion. The RCMP's public communications contain no guidance on how to report suspected Criminal Code violations in MAID contexts.
Q-04
What briefings on MAID expansion were provided to RCMP Commissioners prior to each legislative vote?
Standard government practice requires the RCMP Commissioner to be briefed by the Minister of Public Safety on major Criminal Code amendments. The content of these briefings for C-14 and C-7 has not been made public. Access to Information requests are the appropriate mechanism to pursue this.
Legitimate Accountability Mechanisms

These are the formal accountability pathways available to Canadians regarding MAID policy:

  • House of Commons Petitions — petitions.ourcommons.ca — petitions with 500+ signatures receive a mandatory government response
  • ATIP Requests (RCMP) — rcmp-grc.gc.ca/en/access-information-and-privacy — request internal assessments and briefing notes
  • ATIP Requests (Health Canada) — canada.ca — Health Canada ATIP
  • Senate MAID Committee — sencanada.ca/en/committees/AMAD/ — submit testimony or written submissions
  • UN Special Rapporteur — ohchr.org — individual communications accepted
  • Civilian Review and Complaints Commission for the RCMP — crcc-ccetp.gc.ca — file complaints about RCMP conduct or inaction
