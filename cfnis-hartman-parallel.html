🔍 INVESTIGATION — CFNIS × HARTMAN

One Doctrine. Two Veterans.
The Same Closure.

The same "no private law duty of care" reasoning that let Ontario Superior Court strike Dan Hartman's wrongful-death claim for his 17-year-old son is the reasoning that lets the Canadian Forces National Investigation Service refuse to investigate Daniel Perry's s.504 criminal complaint. One doctrine, two combat veterans' families, the same structural closure.

The doctrine

Justice Sandra Antoniani's trial holding in Hartman v. Canada (Attorney General) (Ontario Superior Court of Justice, March 2025) is the controlling statement of Canadian doctrine on government duty of care for health-emergency policy. The exact words — emphasis added —

"There is no private law duty of care to individual members of the public injured by government core policy decisions in the handling of health emergencies which impact the general population." Justice Sandra Antoniani, Ontario Superior Court of Justice, March 2025, striking Hartman v. Canada (Attorney General). Now on appeal to ONCA (item 24102), oral argument heard January 26 2026.

Read the holding carefully. It does not say vaccines are safe. It does not say the Hartman family's claim is factually wrong. It says: even if everything you allege is true, Canadian tort law gives you no remedy against the government for its policy choices.

If upheld by the Ontario Court of Appeal, this doctrine reaches far beyond vaccine injury. Every Canadian harmed by a government's "core policy decision" — pandemic lockdowns, MAID eligibility expansion, carceral policy, mass surveillance, military ROE — loses the private-law cause of action. The reach is total. That is why this page exists.

Sources: Ontario Court of Appeal item 24102 — coadecisions.ontariocourts.ca/coa/coa/en/item/24102/index.do; Children's Health Defense Canada case summary; Western Standard reporting (Lee Harding, 2024).

Two veterans' families. The same doctrine applied to both.

Sean Hartman

17-year-old, Beeton Ontario, dead 21 days post-vaccination

Facts pleaded: Sean Hartman received a Pfizer-BioNTech mRNA COVID-19 vaccine during Ontario's adolescent rollout. Three weeks later, on September 17 2021, he died suddenly. Tissue samples were examined by Dr. Ryan Cole (US pathologist); findings supported a vaccine-related cause of death.

Who filed: Dan Hartman (father).

Counsel: Umar Sheikh.

Torts pleaded: (a) negligence (Anns/Cooper duty of care); (b) misfeasance in public office.

Outcome at trial: Struck with no leave to amend. No private law duty of care.

What remedies remain: VISP (Vaccine Injury Support Program) — approved 234 of 3,317 claims (7%). Hartman appeal pending 32+ months.

Daniel Perry

Canadian Forces Signals Operator, Afghanistan veteran, Indigenous Canadian

Facts pleaded (per s.504 Information): After reporting suspected foreign interference within the Canadian Armed Forces, Perry was subject to political prosecution by CFNIS under Capt. Rebecca Covey; forced psychiatric confinement and medication; medical records falsified (Dr. Zoe Selhi); business accounts hacked; employment targeted; disclosure withheld in violation of R v Stinchcombe [1991] 3 SCR 326; evidence sanitised (Vicky Bae).

Who filed: Daniel Perry, self-represented.

Instrument: Form 2 Information under s.504 of the Criminal Code — 29 counts against Capt. Rebecca Covey (CFNIS) and others.

NDA sections invoked: s.83, s.92, s.95, s.119, s.124, s.125, s.129, s.130. Criminal Code: s.122, s.137, s.139, s.269.1, s.423.1.

Outcome so far: CFNIS has not investigated. No response to formal notifications. NDA s.83 creates a statutory duty to investigate; non-investigation after notification is itself an offence under NDA s.124.

What remedies remain: s.504 private-prosecution pathway (ongoing via s504-tracker.html), Federal Court, international referral under Rome Statute Art. 7 (prosecution.html).

Why this is not two separate stories

The Hartman trial holding says a government injured Canadian has no private-law cause of action against the government's core policy. The same structural logic animates CFNIS's refusal to investigate Perry: if the underlying policy choice (how CFNIS treats whistleblowers who report foreign interference) is itself a "core policy decision", then no individual complainant has standing to compel investigation. Duty is diffuse; no one is accountable to anyone specific.

The National Defence Act duties CFNIS is refusing to perform

CFNIS is not an unregulated service. The National Defence Act imposes specific, statutory duties on military police. NDA s.83 creates the duty to investigate. NDA s.124 makes failure-to-investigate itself an offence. Perry's s.504 Information cites these and other sections as the counts against Covey et al.

Non-investigation after statutory notification is not "agency discretion." It is, on the face of the NDA, an offence. That is the specific structural fact the Hartman doctrine threatens to normalise: if the Ontario Court of Appeal upholds "no private law duty of care" for government policy decisions, an NDA s.83 duty to investigate may still exist in principle but the complainant has no individual remedy when it is ignored.

Why an ONCA affirm would close the Canadian accountability circuit

The closure mechanics

If ONCA upholds the trial holding in Hartman, Canadian doctrine reads roughly as:

1. Tort law does not give you a claim against the government for policy-caused injury (Hartman).
2. The statutory compensation program that was supposed to be the alternative (VISP) approves 7% of claims (234 / 3,317) and spent twice as much on administration as on payments ($33.7M vs $16.9M per Global News investigation).
3. Statutory duties to investigate (NDA s.83, s.124; similar provisions for police services) are abstract: they bind officers in general but no complainant has individual standing to compel their performance.
4. Criminal Code s.504 private prosecution remains nominally available but is subject to AG stay power (s.579), which has been used to shut down politically-sensitive proceedings throughout Canadian history.

Four remedies theoretically available. Each with a doctrinal off-switch. Perry's s.504 filing is the last of the four, and it is the one CFNIS is refusing to engage with.

What a reversal would mean

If ONCA reverses — if the Anns/Cooper duty-of-care test is recognised as applying to pandemic-era policy choices and the Hartman claim is restored — Canadian citizens regain a tort-law pathway against government for policy-caused harm. That pathway reaches Perry's CFNIS matter too: political prosecution as policy choice becomes actionable, not abstract.

A Supreme Court of Canada leave application is the likely subsequent step. SCC grants leave in 10-12% of applications. The doctrine will be settled by whichever court has the last word.
