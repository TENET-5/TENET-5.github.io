§01 · What 5GW means here

The battlespace is perception

Fifth-generation warfare prioritizes perception, legitimacy, and cognitive effects over kinetic force. If you control trusted information channels, you do not need to win every argument — you need to define which arguments are “serious,” which deaths are “compassionate policy,” and which citizens are “fringe.” A state-funded broadcaster with national reach sits at the centre of that battlespace.

This is continuous with TENET5’s 5GW subversion and media capture files — sharpened on CBC as the apex public vector.

§02 · FACT base (primary / on-record)

Tax dollars, appointments, subsidy stack

FACT F1 · FUNDING

CBC’s principal public money is an annual parliamentary appropriation

Canadian Heritage’s modernization paper states the government grants approximately $1.38 billion to CBC/Radio-Canada (~$33.66 per capita), versus a cited G7 average of ~$62.20 per capita. Treasury Board Estimates tables and contemporaneous reporting place 2024–25 support in the ~$1.3–1.4B class with reported year-over-year increases.

FACT F2 · DEPENDENCY

Government funding is the majority of the corporation’s budget

Public reporting places government funding at approximately 70% of CBC/Radio-Canada’s budget, with the remainder self-generated (advertising, etc.). Majority taxpayer dependency is structural, not incidental.

CBC — federal budget / funding share reporting
FACT F3 · APPOINTMENTS

Government appoints the Board and President & CEO

Heritage’s own paper states the government is solely responsible for leadership appointments (Board and President & CEO), and that this “close association” is the exception rather than the rule versus many peer democracies. Arm’s-length operation is claimed; appointment power remains a soft-control surface.

Heritage — governance / appointments section
FACT F4 · TRUDEAU-ERA REINVESTMENT

Budget 2016: $675M over five years ($150M/year permanent class)

Heritage documents a major reinvestment beginning 2016–17: $75M then $150 million per year (permanent class) to produce Canadian content and expand digital reach — under the Trudeau government.

Heritage — Recent investments / footnotes
FACT F5 · PRIVATE PRESS SUBSIDIES

$595M multi-year journalism subsidy architecture (2019 class)

Beyond CBC, the federal government built a private-news support stack. Peer-reviewed analysis documents a ~$595 million over five years bailout/subsidy package (labour tax credit, digital subscription measures, etc.). Local Journalism Initiative funds hundreds of journalist positions in “underserved” communities. CBC is generally not LJI-eligible — but the stack still state-manages the wider news market.

Edge (2021) Canadian Journal of Communication — $595M subsidies
Canada.ca — Local Journalism Initiative
FACT F6 · LEGISLATIVE ARCHITECTURE

Online Streaming Act + Online News Act restructure the information market

Heritage cites the Online Streaming Act (2023) and Online News Act (2023) as tools that update CBC’s environment and platform obligations — state design of the digital information economy, not a free market accident.

Heritage — Two new laws
FACT F7 · ESCALATION PATH

Proposal: statutory per-capita funding toward G7 average

Heritage proposes moving from annual parliamentary votes toward a statutory per-capita appropriation and higher funding toward G7 averages — locking larger taxpayer obligations across electoral cycles. Contemporaneous reporting described pitches that could nearly double yearly appropriation.

Emergency narrative role

Heritage proposals to expand emergency communications role + high appropriation make CBC preferred amplifier during Emergencies Act-class and public-health crises.
