What the timeline shows.

The bar fills regardless of which party is in power. Bill C-14 (2016) was passed by a Liberal majority. Bill C-233 (2024) was defeated by Liberal + Conservative + Bloc whip together. The procurement contracts to Israeli-tier suppliers were signed under the same Cabinets that announced the pause. The capture loop (Flow C) doesn't have a partisan colour because it was never about party — it was about which institutions hire former Cabinet ministers when they leave office.

Bills that would have closed legs of the pipeline were defeated. Bill C-233 (arms export restriction): defeated 22–295. The Track 2 mental-illness MAID review: delayed three times, never repealed. Indigenous Services' drinking-water gap: $44B needed, $3.86B allocated. Each one of these is a recorded choice not to close a leg. That is the Article II intent test on a calendar.

The pipeline isn't a conspiracy. It's a calendar. The bills are public. The votes are public. The numbers are public. They thought nobody would put them in order.
