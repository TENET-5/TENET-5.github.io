### 1. BELLEVILLE CITY COUNCIL CONTROVERSIES

**Integrity Commissioner Fired After Finding Mayor Breached Code of Conduct (2019-2020)**

The most significant accountability issue in Belleville's recent history involves former Mayor Mitch Panciuk and the integrity commissioner. In October 2019, Integrity Commissioner Tony Fleming released a 16-page report finding Panciuk violated the city's code of conduct in two of three complaints -- specifically by interfering in a city hiring process, directing staff to shortlist unqualified candidates for interviews. Fleming recommended a public reprimand and 30-day pay suspension. Council voted not to implement either penalty. Panciuk called the suggested penalties "overdone."

In August 2020, council voted 5-3 to fire Fleming and cancel the contract with his firm Cunningham Swan. Councillor Chris Malette was expelled from the meeting during the debate and stated on the record that some councillors wanted to hire an integrity commissioner who suited their needs. Those voting to fire: Mayor Panciuk, Bill Sandison, Pat Culhane, Kelly McCaw, Sean Kelly. Voting against: Paul Carr, Ryan Williams, Garnet Thompson. Only one bid was received to replace Fleming as integrity commissioner.

**Strong Mayor Powers Controversy (2023-present)**

Belleville council was internally divided over Ontario's strong mayor powers. Council passed a motion (authored by Councillor Paul Carr) opposing the powers, arguing they undermined democratic checks and balances. However, Mayor Neil Ellis stated he planned to use them to secure provincial funding. The tension reflects a broader concern that council is being sidelined on development decisions. Belleville exceeded its 2023 housing target by 284%, receiving $1.2M from the Building Faster Fund, directed toward the $55M Avonlough Sewage Pumping Station.

### 2. QUINTE WEST COUNCIL ISSUES

**Mayor Jim Harrison Breached Code of Conduct -- Verbal Abuse (2023-2024)**

Quinte West's Integrity Commissioner found Mayor Jim Harrison breached sections 6.1 and 6.2 of the Code of Conduct after an incident following a February 8, 2023 council meeting where members discussed a vacant Sidney Ward seat. The commissioner recommended Harrison issue a public apology and lose 15 days' pay. Harrison accepted the discipline in January 2024. The two integrity commissioner investigations cost nearly $18,000, revealed through a Freedom of Information request by Quinte News.

### 5. BELLEVILLE POLICE SERVICE

**Three Officers Charged After Violent Arrest of Mohawk Man (2019-2024)**

This is the highest-profile police accountability case. On November 15, 2019, three Belleville police officers (Constables Kyle Dodds, Paul Fyke, Jeffrey Smith) arrested Mario Baptiste Jr., a 38-year-old Mohawk man and gas station owner from Tyendinaga Mohawk Territory, inside a Taco Bell on North Front Street. The arrest stemmed from an off-duty investigation by Fyke into a suspected stolen energy drink from Lowe's -- Baptiste had purchased $33 in merchandise.

Baptiste lost consciousness during the takedown and sustained a cracked rib and broken finger. All three were charged with assault causing bodily harm by the SIU in May 2021. At trial in October-December 2023: charges against Dodds were withdrawn by the Crown pre-trial; Fyke was acquitted; Smith was acquitted of assault causing bodily harm but found guilty of simple assault (lacking grounds for arrest). In July 2024, Smith received only a conditional discharge.

Baptiste filed a $550,000 civil lawsuit against the Police Service, former Chief Ron Gignac, and four constables. The Crown has filed a Notice of Appeal seeking to overturn Fyke's acquittal and Smith's reduced conviction. CBC reporting also raised questions about delays in the SIU investigation.

### 6. LOCAL PROCUREMENT

**$55M Avonlough Sewage Pumping Station**

The largest current infrastructure project is the Avonlough Sewage Pumping Station ($55M), supporting approximately 9,000 future housing units in west Belleville. Funding sources include reserve funds, development charges, grants, $16.1M from the Canada Housing Infrastructure Fund, taxation debt, and user rate debt. Phase one begins spring 2026. While no specific sole-source or cost overrun controversies were found, the project's scale and multi-year timeline warrant monitoring.

Water and wastewater rates are increasing 5.83% ($7.47/month) for average residential customers in 2025.

### 7. LOYALIST COLLEGE

This is one of the most active accountability stories in the region.

**Massive Program Cuts and Faculty Layoffs (2024-2025)**

Starting in fall 2024, Loyalist has lost 68 of 141 full-time faculty positions (48.2%). In March 2025, 24 programs were suspended and staff cut by 20%. By August 2025, another 36 faculty were laid off. The college blames reduced federal international student enrollment and insufficient provincial funding.

**Privatization Controversy**

The union flagged that Loyalist eliminated its Manufacturing program, only for a privatized version to open weeks later funded by a $1.275M Skills Development Fund grant. The Ontario Auditor General found over half of SDF funding went to applicants ranked "poor" or "mid-level" by government assessors. OPSEU president called it an accountability issue.

**Capital Spending While Cutting Programs**

Union leadership questioned the college spending $17.5M (2026), $33.5M (2025), $23M (2024), and $18M (2023) on building enhancements while gutting academic programs -- including a new front entrance and gym while cutting programs like Esthetics and Spa Management.

**Five-Week Strike (September-October 2025)**

Full-time support staff at all 24 Ontario colleges struck for five weeks. A tentative deal was reached October 15 through mediation by William Kaplan, with staff returning October 16.

### 8. QUINTE HEALTH (HOSPITAL)

**Chronic Underfunding and Deficit**

Quinte Health is running a projected $9M deficit (up from $6.6M in 2023/2024). CUPE reports a 75% failure rate in admitting ER patients within target time. The union calculates Prince Edward County Memorial Hospital needs 18 additional staff and 2 beds per year, Trenton Memorial needs 15 staff and 2 beds, North Hastings needs 6 staff and 1 bed. There are 250 vacancies across 2,200 positions at the four hospitals. North Hastings faces the highest risk of intermittent ER closures.

Municipal healthcare spending has become a local flashpoint -- Quinte West alone spends $746,600 on healthcare partnerships (nearly 1% of total levy) to compensate for provincial shortfalls. South East Public Health announced closure of several locations including Quinte West, raising concerns about service delivery.

### 9. CFB TRENTON

**Base Commander Charged for Shooting at Wildlife (2023-2024)**

Colonel Leif Dahl, Commander of 8 Wing and CFB Trenton, was charged by OPP on August 25, 2023 after shooting at protected wildlife from a boat on the Murray Canal in Quinte West. He had a .22 rifle and a revolver aboard. He was charged with careless use of a firearm, obstructing police, and three Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act violations. He was permanently removed from command on September 28, 2023.

In March 2024, Dahl pleaded guilty to carelessly storing firearms and public mischief, receiving only a conditional discharge -- 12 months probation, firearms ban (except military duties), and a $500 donation to Ducks Unlimited. No criminal record after probation.

No separate CFNIS investigation at CFB Trenton was identified in searches.

### 10. LOCAL REAL ESTATE / DEVELOPMENT

**Village of Avonlea Land Speculation**

Councillor Paul Carr publicly flagged that a developer received rezoning for a 750-home project (Village of Avonlea), and almost immediately listed the land for sale at $29 million. Carr argued this exemplified land speculation rather than housing construction. He brought a motion to force builders to divulge costs, but it failed at council in November 2024.

**Housing Affordability Crisis**

Between 2015 and 2024, average apartment rents increased 56% (from $918 to $1,435/month). There are 3,125 households on the waitlist for non-market housing, and 229 individuals were identified as experiencing homelessness. Belleville needs approximately 3,990 new permanent housing units over the 2025-2035 period. Despite record approvals, housing starts by the private sector declined in 2023.

**Federal Funding**

Belleville received $10.5M in federal housing funding to help construct 259 homes over three years, plus $16.1M from the Canada Housing Infrastructure Fund for the Avonlough sewage project supporting west-end development.
