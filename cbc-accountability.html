Media accountability

CBC / Radio-Canada — Accountability File

A public broadcaster funded by taxpayers should be the most, not the least, accountable newsroom in the country. This file documents how CBC/Radio-Canada is structured, funded, and governed — and the official channels through which its journalism is reviewed. Every claim links to a primary or official source. Where a specific figure or decision is still being verified against its source, it is marked as such rather than asserted.

Status & mandate

CBC/Radio-Canada is a federal Crown corporation. Its mandate, structure, and public-broadcaster obligations are set out in the Broadcasting Act (S.C. 1991, c. 11). As a Crown corporation it reports to Parliament through the responsible minister, and its corporate documents are published on its own site.

Sources: Broadcasting Act — Justice Laws · CBC/Radio-Canada — Governance

Public funding

The corporation is funded primarily by an annual parliamentary appropriation, supplemented by advertising and other revenue. The exact appropriation and total revenue for each fiscal year are published in CBC/Radio-Canada's Annual Report and in the federal Estimates. Governance

CBC/Radio-Canada is directed by a Board of Directors. Under the Broadcasting Act, the directors and the President and Chief Executive Officer are appointed by the Governor in Council (Cabinet). This appointment structure — a government-appointed board and CEO at a newsroom the government also funds — is itself a recurring subject of accountability debate, and is documented on the public record rather than alleged.

Sources: Broadcasting Act — appointment provisions · CBC/Radio-Canada — Board of Directors

Regulation — the CRTC

CBC/Radio-Canada is regulated by the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) and operates under conditions set at licence renewal. CRTC renewal decisions — including the specific conditions imposed on the public broadcaster — are published in full and are searchable on the CRTC's site. Decision number verification in progress.

Sources: CRTC · CRTC — broadcasting decisions

How CBC's journalism is reviewed

CBC's news operates under a published editorial policy, the Journalistic Standards and Practices (JSP). Complaints about English-language content are reviewed independently by the Office of the Ombudsman, which publishes its review decisions. A review can find that specific coverage breached the JSP; those findings are the highest-credibility evidence of a failure in CBC's journalism, because they are the broadcaster's own accountability mechanism reaching that conclusion.

This file tracks published ombudsman reviews that upheld a complaint, and on-the-record corrections and retractions — each linked to the decision itself. What this file does — and does not — claim

This is media-accountability reporting, held to the same standard as the rest of TENET5: every claim traces to a primary or official source. It documents public facts — funding, governance, regulation, and the broadcaster's own published review findings.

It does not assert that CBC coordinates online harassment, targets any group, or runs a covert campaign. Those are claims that cannot be established from public sources, and naming private individuals in connection with them would be both unverifiable and defamatory. Where the public record shows a documented editorial failure — an upheld ombudsman review, a correction, a CRTC finding — this file will cite it directly. Speculation is not published here.
