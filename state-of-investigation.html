Hypotheses (framings the data supports, not yet proven)

Hypothesis · structural No Canadian federal oversight body has jurisdiction over cross-axis policy-weighting decisions by ministers with dual policy-domain affiliation. The Wilson-Raybould dual-axis finding (indigenous + judicial) identifies this gap. The Conflict of Interest Act checks financial conflicts. The Ethics Commissioner checks section 9 breaches. The Canadian Judicial Council reviews already-appointed judges. None checks whether an AG's policy-domain affiliations weighted their appointment recommendations. The Wilson-Raybould page develops this framing.
Hypothesis · monetary Central-banking decisions during the 2020–2022 window materially shaped Canadian household balance sheets with less parliamentary scrutiny than fiscal decisions of comparable magnitude. The 16th axis surfaces 6 marked actors — all unelected. The Bank of Canada balance sheet expanded from ~120B CAD to >550B CAD in ~24 months. Scrutinized formally by AG 2022 and PBO, but largely outside the 14-axis scope before LIRIL's R5 advice added the axis. Testable against BoC Monetary Policy Reports and OSFI records.
Hypothesis · dispatch effectiveness Pre-built .eml files with named-sender citizen letters outperform mass-mailer campaigns on oversight-body response rate. Untested. The dispatch chain is ready; no letters have been sent. LIRIL in R4 suggested a specific sequence (Monday 9 AM RCMP+Judicial → Tuesday Foreign+Climate → etc.) but this is theoretical until tested.

Single most important structural finding

The Canadian federal accountability system distributes responsibility across independent oversight bodies so thoroughly that no body has jurisdiction over cross-axis patterns.

Each axis has its designated oversight: the Ethics Commissioner for conflict-of-interest breaches, the Auditor General for spending, the Canadian Judicial Council for judicial conduct, the Privacy Commissioner for data handling, the Information Commissioner for transparency, the Commissioner of the Environment and Sustainable Development for climate commitments, the Parliamentary Budget Officer for costed decisions, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions for prudential regulation, CRCC for RCMP conduct, NSICOP and NSIRA for national security.

When one actor appears across multiple of these axes — as Trudeau does in 9, as LeBlanc / Mendicino / Lametti do in 3 each, as Sajjan / Blair / Wilson-Raybould do in 2 — no single body has the jurisdiction or the incentive to examine the cross-axis pattern itself.

The cross-axis analysis reveals this because it operates on the pattern of actors and axes together, which is the pattern none of the individual oversight bodies is designed to see. That is the thesis: the accountability gap is not a specific missed failure; it is a structural blindspot in the oversight architecture itself.

Open questions

  • Canadian Surface Combatant: LIRIL R3 surfaced CSC as the strongest-paper-trail / weakest-media-coverage issue; R4 drafted a PBO letter (shipped) but a dedicated 17th-axis dossier is not yet built. Primary sources ready: PBO 2021 CSC cost analysis, AG reports on NSS, DND/PSPC ATIP memos.
  • Dispatch response rates: with 15 .eml files committed and none transmitted, the core hypothesis about citizen-accountability effectiveness remains untested. Needs: at least one full cohort of campaigns sent + response-rate tracking by oversight body.
  • Temporal overlap: cross-axis bridges are currently scored by axis count alone. Adding a time-dimension (coverage windows per actor) would show when multi-axis actors' tenures overlapped — a stricter cross-axis signal. Primary sources: Canada Gazette appointment notices, cabinet shuffle records.
