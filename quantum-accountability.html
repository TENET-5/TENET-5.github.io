06 Methodology & Limitations

The TENET5 quantum bridge uses numpy statevector simulation on CPU. It is not a physical quantum computer. On a 4-qubit or 11-qubit register, classical brute force is trivial — Grover's quadratic speedup is not materially faster on this scale. The value of running real quantum algorithms on real data is:

Formula Correction · 2026-04-17
Grover iteration count: exact arcsin form
The initial bridge used the small-angle approximation k ≈ ⌊π/4·√(N/M)⌋ for optimal iterations. At M/N = 1/4 (the arms-pipeline case with 4 marked out of 16), that approximation over-rotates and returns to the classical baseline at k=2. The corrected formula k = round(π/(4·arcsin(√(M/N))) − 1/2) picks k=1, which is the theoretical optimum (success probability = 1.0 exactly). After the fix, all three decision-maker oracles achieved ≥93% success — up from ~60% with the old formula. Arms went from 26.7% (noise around classical baseline) to 100.0%. Network sweep amplifications up to 666.7× for the smallest M/N ratios.
All *_grover_decisionmakers.json files regenerated 2026-04-17; Merkles updated in Section 5 above.
  • Algorithmic transparency. The oracle IS the investigative claim. An oracle that marks "tenure overlaps ≥ 4 canonical failure events" makes the accountability thesis first-class and testable.
  • Portability to hardware. The same circ_grover_search function runs unchanged on GPU-accelerated simulators and on real NISQ hardware (IBM Quantum, IonQ, Rigetti) given a backend adapter. Architectural readiness matters more than present-day speed.
  • Amplification as structural audit. When five orthogonal type-filter oracles amplify the same named actors, the convergence is structural — not a classification artifact of any single axis.

Explicit limitations: the corrections dossier has 16 actors by construction (4 qubits). Expanding the roster would require a 5-qubit register (32 slots). The network address space is capped at 2^11 = 2,048 by current bridge configuration; the full 1,181-node network fits. Scaling to larger investigations would need either larger quantum registers or a Bayesian batch-wise approach.
