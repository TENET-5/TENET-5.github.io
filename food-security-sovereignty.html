Three Food Failures

Market Concentration — 3 Companies, Your Food Supply

As documented in the grocery oligopoly analysis, Loblaws/Weston, Sobeys/Empire, and Metro control 60%+ of the grocery market. They fixed bread prices for 14 years. Their profits hit record highs during the cost-of-living crisis. The Competition Bureau's response was modest. The Grocery Code of Conduct was resisted. Three boardrooms determine what Canadians eat and what they pay — with minimal competitive pressure and captured regulatory oversight.

Affordability Crisis — Housing Consumes Food Budgets

When housing costs 50%+ of income in major cities, food budgets are the first casualty. Families choose between rent and nutrition. As documented in the child welfare crisis, 1 in 4 children experience food insecurity. The housing financialization that drives unaffordability directly produces food insecurity — the two crises are connected through the family budget. The international-law and crime-reduction angle on this same nutritional baseline — Canada's binding ICESCR Art. 11 obligation, and the peer-reviewed randomised-controlled evidence that meeting nutritional baseline cuts violent offending by ~35% — is documented as its own dossier.

Northern Food Sovereignty — 2-3x Southern Prices

Northern and Indigenous communities pay 2-3x southern food prices due to transportation costs, limited competition, and inadequate supply chains. As documented in the treaty violations analysis, the government that claims sovereignty over the Arctic and northern territories fails to ensure affordable food for the people living there. The Nutrition North program provides subsidies but has been criticized for benefiting retailers rather than reducing consumer prices. Northern food sovereignty is another dimension of the Arctic sovereignty failure.

The 9th Dimension of Captured Sovereignty

The sovereignty summary documents 10 dimensions of failure. Food sovereignty is the 9th: a nation that produces abundant agriculture but cannot ensure its population eats affordably because 3 companies control the market, housing costs consume budgets, and northern communities are structurally underserved.

You cannot choose not to eat. When 3 companies control your food and housing consumes your budget, every meal is a transaction with a captured market funded by what's left after rent.
