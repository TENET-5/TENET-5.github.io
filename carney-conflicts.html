The $50 Billion Maple Fund

In September 2024, while Mark Carney was serving as economic adviser to Prime Minister Trudeau, his company Brookfield Asset Management pitched a $50 billion "Maple Fund" — asking for $10 billion from the federal government and $36 billion from Canada's largest pension funds.

The Conflict

Carney was simultaneously: (1) advising the PM on economic policy, and (2) board chair of the company pitching for $50B in public money. The fund would invest in housing, data centres, nuclear energy, and telecommunications — all sectors the PM's adviser would have influence over.

The Ethics Screen That Covers 5%

Carney set up an ethics screen administered by his chief of staff and the Privy Council Clerk. But neither administrator knows the specific assets in the Brookfield Global Transition Fund — from which Carney stands to draw future bonus pay potentially in the tens of millions.

95% Unscreened

95% of Brookfield's portfolio companies are NOT subject to the PM's ethics screen. The screen has been applied only 6 times after 13 potential conflicts were flagged. Carney placed assets in a blind trust — meaning he still owns them, he just can't direct them.

The BlackRock Appointment

On March 4, 2026, Carney appointed a BlackRock executive as Deputy Minister of International Trade — after just 13 months at BlackRock. BlackRock is a co-investor and shareholder with Brookfield.

The Revolving Door

The PM appoints someone from a company that co-invests with his former company to run the country's international trade portfolio. This is the revolving door operating at the highest level of government.

NorthRiver Midstream — 375% Surge in 2023

Brookfield subsidiary NorthRiver Midstream went from 12 comms (2022) to 57 comms (2023) — a 375% surge. This is the same year Carney was advising Trudeau and the Maple Fund was being pitched. In April 2026, NorthRiver Midstream lobbied PM Carney directly. And in Washington DC, Carney met with the CEO of Brookfield Infrastructure. When your board chair becomes PM, you don't need many registered lobbyists — you have direct access.

The India Flip-Flop

In 2024, the RCMP accused Indian diplomats of "serious criminal activity including homicides" in Canada. The Bishnoi gang was listed as a terrorist entity for acting "on behalf of the Indian government."

Then in March 2026, RCMP Commissioner Duheme said there's "no direct link" to a foreign entity in current investigations — contradicting both CSIS (which still lists India as a primary actor) and the RCMP's own previous statements.

The Timing

The RCMP walked back its accusations ahead of Carney's India trade visit. Government officials were documented downplaying India allegations before the visit.
