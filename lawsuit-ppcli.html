01 Nature of the Claim

This action arises from the systemic failure of the Canadian Armed Forces, the Department of National Defence, and specifically the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI) to fulfill their legal duties of care to a combat veteran.

The plaintiff, Corporal Daniel Perry (Retired), served as a Signals Operator in the Canadian Forces. This statement of claim documents a pattern of institutional negligence, reprisal against a whistleblower, and the deliberate failure to provide adequate medical care, support, and administrative justice to a member who reported systemic misconduct.

Criminal Code of Canada, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46

Charge I: Breach of Duty of Care

The Crown, as employer, owed a duty of care to ensure the health, safety, and welfare of its serving members. This duty was breached through systemic neglect, administrative obstruction, and wilful failure to act on documented medical conditions.

National Defence Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. N-5, s. 129

Charge II: Conduct to the Prejudice of Good Order and Discipline

Senior officers and NCOs within the chain of command engaged in conduct prejudicial to good order by: suppressing internal complaints, retaliating against whistleblowing activities, and creating a hostile command environment designed to force administrative discharge rather than address systemic failures.

Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, s. 7, 15

Charge III: Violation of Charter Rights

The systematic denial of adequate medical care, the obstruction of access to justice through the military grievance system, and the discriminatory treatment of injured veterans constitutes a violation of the plaintiff's right to life, liberty, security of the person (s. 7), and equality rights (s. 15).

Canadian Human Rights Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. H-6

Charge IV: Systemic Discrimination

The institutional response to the plaintiff's injuries, complaints, and appeals reveals a pattern of discrimination on the basis of disability and veteran status. The system is designed to exhaust, discourage, and administratively bury legitimate claims.

Public Servants Disclosure Protection Act, S.C. 2005, c. 46

Charge V: Whistleblower Reprisal

Following the plaintiff's disclosure of institutional misconduct, a documented pattern of reprisal emerged including: reassignment, denial of medical appointments, administrative harassment, and obstruction of career progression — all constituting prohibited reprisal under federal law.

02 Facts Alleged

The following material facts are alleged in support of the claim:

  • The plaintiff served honourably in combat operations as a Signals Operator
  • The plaintiff sustained service-related injuries requiring ongoing medical treatment
  • The chain of command was repeatedly notified of the plaintiff's medical condition and administrative concerns
  • Despite repeated notifications, the institution failed to provide adequate medical care or administrative remedy
  • The plaintiff reported systemic misconduct through proper channels
  • Following these reports, a pattern of reprisal and administrative obstruction was documented
  • The military grievance system was used as a tool of delay rather than a mechanism of justice
  • The CFNIS (Canadian Forces National Investigation Service) investigation revealed systemic failures but no institutional accountability followed
  • Multiple other veterans have experienced substantially similar treatment, establishing a pattern and practice

03 Relief Sought

  1. Declaration that the Crown breached its duty of care to the plaintiff
  2. Declaration that the Crown engaged in prohibited whistleblower reprisal
  3. General damages for pain, suffering, and loss of quality of life
  4. Special damages for documented medical expenses, lost income, and career impact
  5. Punitive damages reflecting the systemic nature of the misconduct and the institutional refusal to self-correct
  6. Mandatory order requiring the implementation of systemic reforms to the military grievance and medical support systems
  7. Costs on a full indemnity basis

04 Proceeding Timeline

2022
Internal Grievances Exhausted — All internal military grievance mechanisms were pursued and proved inadequate. The system functioned as designed: to delay, obstruct, and exhaust.
2023
CFNIS Investigation — The Canadian Forces National Investigation Service investigated and documented systemic failures. Despite findings, no meaningful institutional accountability resulted.
2024
Legal Counsel Retained — Civilian legal counsel engaged to pursue federal court proceedings after exhaustion of all military administrative remedies.
2025
Statement of Claim Filed — Formal action commenced before the Federal Court of Canada. The Crown has been served.
