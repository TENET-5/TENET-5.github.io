Narrative Layer — The Architecture of Parallel Corporate Influence

The web is not accidental. Across 17 accountability axes, the 44 flagged decision-makers and their families maintain layered control through Brookfield Asset Management, Power Corporation of Canada, dozens of numbered companies, private trusts, and interlocked board seats. Public records (SEDI, SEDAR+, Lobbying Registry, OECC) show the same names recurring in policy-influencing entities at the exact moments major decisions were made on energy, infrastructure, and social programs.

Timeline of concentrated appointments. Between 2015-2023, multiple flagged individuals moved into senior roles at Crown corporations, regulatory bodies, and pension funds managing hundreds of billions. The pattern mirrors the "revolving door" documented in the MAID and opioid axes — influence flows in one direction during policy formation, then back to private vehicles that directly benefit from the outcomes.

Geography of control. While holdings appear national, the densest nodes sit in Ontario (Queen's Park and federal Ottawa overlap), Quebec (Power Corp historic base), and Alberta energy vehicles. The same family offices and numbered companies appear in municipal infrastructure deals, provincial energy files, and federal lobbying records — a multi-level parallel pipeline.

Follow the numbered companies. Many of the most sensitive positions are held through private numbered entities (e.g., 1234567 Ontario Ltd.) whose beneficial owners are only visible through cross-referenced SEDI filings, lobbying registrations, and occasional court or regulatory disclosures. This structure creates the same opacity the project has documented in other axes.

The accountability gap is structural. Public ethics rules capture direct employment and certain gifts, but layered trusts, family holding companies, and "advisory" roles in vehicles that do not trigger disclosure create a second, parallel system of influence. The data in the cards below is the visible surface. The full architecture requires the same forensic cross-referencing the project has applied to MAID, opioids, and foreign interference.

Methodology & limits

  • Public record only. Every holding entry cites at least one publicly-available source: OECC public registry, federal Lobbying Registry, SEDI insider filings, SEDAR+ continuous disclosure, Order in Council, or major news of record (Globe and Mail, CBC, CTV, Reuters, Bloomberg, Wall Street Journal).
  • Inclusion criterion. A holding is listed only when it constitutes a corporate interest, trust beneficiary position, board seat, executive role, partnership, advisory engagement, or significant shareholding of accountability relevance. Personal residences, ordinary banking, and small non-controlling investment-fund holdings are not listed.
  • Empty holdings lists. A principal with holdings=[] is not a claim of "no business interest." It means no publicly-disclosed holding of accountability relevance has been catalogued for this principal yet.
  • No accusation. Listing a holding does not assert wrongdoing. The registry documents the corporate / trust topology around flagged principals so readers can independently assess conflict-of-interest surface area on the public record.
  • Reproducible. Generated by the automated dossier generator (seeded, reproducible). Output: data/business_holdings_dossier.json. The page is a direct render of that file.
